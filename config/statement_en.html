  Goal

Barricade is a port of the turn-based board game Quoridor. Advance your pawn, block your rival with barriers, and win by reaching your goal zone first.

The referee source code can be found: here.

  Rules

The game is played on a grid.

Each turn a player can perform either a MOVE or a BARRIER action.

If neither player reaches their goal zone in the turn limit, the game is considered a draw.

🎬  Actions

The two actions that are available each turn are: MOVE x y or BARRIER ORIENTATION x y
The MOVE command is used to move a player's pawn around the grid.
The BARRIER command is used to place barriers on the grid.

🏃🏽  Movement Rules

To move a pawn, a player uses the MOVE x y command. The coordinates (x, y) refer to the tile where the player wants their pawn to move to. The top-left corner is positioned at coordinates (0,0) and the bottom-right is positioned at coordinates (w-1,h-1).

Basic Movement:
Players can move their pawn one space orthogonally (UP, DOWN, LEFT, or RIGHT) into an adjacent empty tile. The pawn cannot pass through/over a placed barrier or go outside the grid boundaries. Only one player can occupy any single tile at any point. In the event the opponent blocks a player's pawn from moving in a single orthogonal direction, they may be able to move via jumping or diagonally.

Basic orthogonal movements available.

Basic orthogonal movements available as a result of restricted movement caused by barriers.

Jumping over an Opponent:
When your pawn is directly adjacent to the opponent's pawn (orthogonally), you may jump over them into the tile directly behind them using a single MOVE command. You cannot jump over an opponent if there is a barrier between you and them, a barrier directly behind them, or if the jump would move your pawn outside the grid boundaries.

Example of jump movement options. Black bars indicate the edge of the grid.

Diagonal Movement:
If a straight jump over the opponent is blocked by a barrier directly behind the opponent or the edge of the grid, they may instead make a diagonal move. A diagonal move allows you to step into either of the two tiles adjacent to the opponent's pawn, provided no barrier blocks the path between the opponent's pawn and that diagonal space. Note: You cannot make a diagonal move if the straight jump behind the opponent is completely clear.

Example of the diagonal movement options. Black bars indicate the edge of the grid.

🚧  Barrier Rules

Barriers are placed onto the grid to prevent players from moving between the tiles it blocks. Each player has a total of 10 barriers that they have access to place during a game. There is no restriction on the number of each orientation of barriers, a player can be use any combination of vertical and horizontal barriers.

A player can place a vertical or horizontal barrier onto the grid by using: BARRIER ORIENTATION x y. ORIENTATION is either VERT which indicates a vertical barrier or HOR which indicates a horizontal barrier.

Barrier Coordinate System:
Barriers occupy the grooves between tiles and always span a length of exactly 2 tiles. Placement coordinates (x, y) refer to the barrier's starting point:

  • Vertical Barrier (VERT): Placed along the right edge of tiles (x,y) and (x,y+1), separating column x from column x+1.
  • Horizontal Barrier (HOR): Placed along the bottom edge of tiles (x,y) and (x+1,y), separating row y from row y+1.

Valid coordinates for placing a barrier range from x=0 to w-2 and y=0 to h-2.

Barrier Placement Rules:

  • No Overlap: A barrier cannot be placed directly or partially on top of an existing barrier segment. Nor can a vertical barrier and a horizontal barrier cannot share the same midpoint point.
  • No Complete Blocking: A barrier cannot completely block either player from reaching their respective goal row.

🥅  Goal Zone

Your goal zone is the row of tiles on the opposite side of the grid from where your pawn starts. The objective is to move your pawn into any tile within the goal zone. If a players starting pawns row is y=0, their goal zone is y=h-1. Alternatively, if a players starting pawns row is y=h-1, their goal zone is y=0.

Victory Conditions
  • Your pawn reaches your goal zone before the opponents pawn reach their goal zone.
Loss Conditions
  • Your program does not provide a command in the alloted time or one of the commands is invalid.

🐞 Debug Modes

Enable the debug mode using the settings icon to provide extra information when hovering over tiles and barriers in the viewer.

Additonally, enable possible moves mode to highlight all tiles a player can move to each turn.

  Game Input

Initialization Input

Line 1: Space separated integers h w representing the height and width of the grid.

Line 2: Space separated integers x1 y1 representing the starting coordinates of YOUR pawn.

Line 3: Space separated integers x2 y2 representing the starting coordinates of the OPPONENT'S pawn.

Input per turn

A single line: The action performed by the other player during their turn, in one of the forms:

  • MOVE x y - This indicates the other player moved their pawn to the coordinates (x,y).
  • BARRIER orientation x y - This indicates the other player placed a barrier starting at coordinates (x,y) in the direction indicated by orientation.

The first turn input will be the string (null) which indicates this player has the first turn.

Output per turn
A single line of one of the available actions:
  • MOVE x y - Where the integers x and y indicates the coordinates (x,y) for the player to move their pawn to.
  • BARRIER orientation x y - Places a barrier starting at coordinates (x,y) in the direction indicated by orientation.
Constraints
? ≤ h, w ≤ ?
orientation is either VERT or HOR.
First turn response time limit is 1000 ms.
Subsequent turn response time limit is 150 ms.
Turn limit is 200 turns.
